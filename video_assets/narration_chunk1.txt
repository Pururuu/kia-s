Imagine knowing the universe is wrong. Not slightly wrong. Completely wrong. Every scientist believes it. Every professor teaches it. And somehow... I'm the only one who can see it. But right now... Nobody thinks I'm special. My teachers certainly don't. They want answers. I only have questions. Questions are dangerous here. They make adults uncomfortable. Why does light move the way it does? What is time? What is gravity? And why does reality behave the way it does? Then one day... My father shows me a compass. The needle moved. But nothing touched it. No strings. No hands. No visible force. That moment haunted him. If invisible forces existed, what else was hiding beneath reality? That question would consume the rest of his life. Fast forward. Einstein isn't a famous scientist. He's not even a university professor. He's a clerk. Every day. The same desk. The same papers. But inside his mind, something extraordinary was happening. He began running thought experiments. Imagine riding a beam of light. What would you see? Nobody knew. Because nobody had ever asked. Then he asked another question. What if time isn't fixed? What if it changes? At the time, this sounded ridiculous. Time was time. In 1905, everything changed. Einstein published four papers. Not one. Four. Each capable of making someone famous. Together they transformed physics forever. He showed that space and time were connected. Not separate. One fabric. Mass bends space. And that bending is what we call gravity. Imagine discovering that reality itself works differently than everyone believes.
